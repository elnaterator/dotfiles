# Weekly summary email template

Structure for the end-of-week accomplishments email. Fill it from the synthesized worklog entries,
then hand the result to **write-like-me** so the final wording lands in the user's voice.

**How to use it**
- Sections are a checklist, not a form. Drop any section with no real content — an empty "Blockers"
  heading reads worse than no heading.
- 3–6 highlights total across all themes. This is a summary, not a replay of the week.
- Every bullet answers "so what?" — outcome and who benefited, not the mechanics.
- Never invent content to fill a section. Thin week → short email.
- Placeholders are `{{like this}}`.

---

**Subject:** Weekly update — {{Name}} — week of {{Mon DD}}

Hi {{recipient}},

{{One or two sentences framing the week: the headline outcome, or the theme that ties the work
together. Lead with the thing that mattered most. Skip if the bullets speak for themselves.}}

**{{Theme / project A}}**
- {{Accomplishment framed by impact — outcome + who it unblocked or benefited.}}
- {{...}}

**{{Theme / project B}}**
- {{...}}

**In flight**
- {{Work underway with where it stands and expected landing — only if genuinely notable.}}

**Blockers / needs**
- {{What's stuck and the specific help or decision needed, with the owner. Omit if nothing is
  blocked — don't manufacture an ask.}}

**Next week**
- {{1–3 items of intended focus. Omit if it's just "continue the above".}}

{{Sign-off}}
{{Name}}

---

## Variants

- **Manager 1:1 / status update** — keep themes and blockers, drop the greeting and sign-off, tighten
  to bullets only.
- **Wide audience (skip-level, cross-team)** — lead with outcomes in business terms, cut internal
  implementation detail and team-specific jargon, keep it to the top 3 highlights.
- **Quarterly / multi-week roll-up** — replace day-level detail entirely with themes; each bullet
  covers a body of work rather than a single accomplishment.
